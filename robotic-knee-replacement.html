Introduction

Knee pain that limits your daily life deserves more than a temporary fix. Dr. Vaibhav Bagaria, Director of Orthopaedics at Sir H. N. Reliance Foundation Hospital, performs robotic knee replacement in Mumbai using robotic-arm assistance that helps position the new joint to your individual knee rather than an average one.

He is among the earliest surgeons in India to adopt this technique, and treats patients travelling from across Mumbai and Maharashtra — including knees that have become stiff over the years, both knees together, and knees where an earlier replacement needs to be redone.

What is robotic knee replacement?

Knee replacement removes the worn surfaces of the knee and replaces them with new joint components. The bone underneath is preserved — only the damaged surface layer is taken away.

What robotic assistance adds is planning and accuracy. Before the operation, a plan is made for your specific knee: the size and position of each component, and how the ligaments will be balanced. During surgery, the robotic arm helps the surgeon work to that plan within a defined boundary.

The surgeon performs the operation. The robotic arm is an instrument, not an operator — it does not make decisions and it does not work independently.

Who this procedure is for

Robotic knee replacement suits patients whose arthritis has reached the point where daily life is meaningfully limited, and where physiotherapy, medication and activity changes have taken you as far as they can.

It is generally the right option when:

  • ✓ Arthritis affects the whole knee
  • ✓ Pain limits walking, stairs and everyday activities
  • ✓ Sleep is disturbed by knee pain
  • ✓ Non-surgical treatment is no longer giving enough relief
  • ✓ X-rays show advanced wear of the joint
  • ✓ You want to return to walking comfortable distances again

Where arthritis is confined to one part of the knee , partial knee replacement may suit you better — it preserves more of your own knee and recovers faster. Dr. Bagaria will advise which is right after examining you and reviewing your X-rays.

The X-ray is only part of the picture. What matters most is how much the knee is limiting the life you want to live.

How robotic assistance changes the procedure

The difference is in the planning, and in how consistently that plan is carried out.

Alignment

How the components are positioned affects how the knee feels and how evenly load passes through it. Planning this in advance rather than judging it during surgery improves consistency.

Soft tissue balance

The ligaments on either side of the knee need to be evenly tensioned. Assessing this through the range of movement helps get the balance right.

Sizing

Component size is planned to your knee before the operation begins.

Where it helps most is in the harder cases — stiff knees with limited movement, significant deformity, and revision surgery. Dr. Bagaria has published research specifically on robotic knee replacement in stiff knees.

Things to discuss with your surgeon

Every operation is a decision, and it should be an informed one. Dr. Bagaria discusses all of the following with you before you consent, and there is no such thing as too many questions.

  • ✓ What results you can realistically expect, given your knee, your age and your activity
  • ✓ How long the implant is expected to last in your circumstances
  • ✓ What the recovery will ask of you, and what support you will need at home
  • ✓ How pain will be managed during your stay and afterwards
  • ✓ What precautions apply in the early weeks
  • ✓ How your other medical conditions are managed around surgery
  • ✓ What follow-up looks like, and why long-term review is advised
  • ✓ What the alternatives are, including waiting

Bring a family member to the consultation if you can. It helps to have someone else hearing the answers, and questions often come afterwards rather than in the room.

Results — what to expect

Most patients gain substantial pain relief and considerably better function.

  • ✓ Walking comfortable distances again is the most common improvement patients describe
  • ✓ Stairs, getting in and out of a car, and sleeping without pain typically return
  • ✓ Modern implants function well for many years, with longevity depending on age, weight, activity and implant type
  • ✓ Low-impact activity such as walking, cycling and swimming is encouraged long term
  • ✓ Long-term follow-up is advised so the joint can be reviewed periodically

Your surgeon will discuss what to expect for your knee specifically, including guidance on kneeling, squatting and cross-legged sitting.
