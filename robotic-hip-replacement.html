Introduction

Hip pain has a way of shrinking your world — a shorter walk, help needed with shoes, a stride that has quietly become a limp. Dr. Vaibhav Bagaria performs robotic hip replacement in Mumbai at Sir H. N. Reliance Foundation Hospital, for arthritis, avascular necrosis and hips stiffened by long-standing conditions or old injury.

The aim is straightforward: a hip that moves freely, legs that feel the same length, and walking that feels normal again rather than merely possible.

What is hip replacement?

The hip is a ball-and-socket joint. In replacement, the worn ball at the top of the thigh bone is replaced and the socket is resurfaced with a new cup, giving a smooth surface for the joint to move on again.

Three measurements determine how the hip feels afterwards: where the cup sits, how equal the leg lengths are, and the offset — how far the joint sits from the centre of the body, which affects muscle tension and stability.

Planning all three in advance, and executing to that plan, is what robotic assistance helps with.

Who this procedure is for

Hip replacement suits patients whose hip pain and stiffness have begun to limit walking, sleep and everyday activities, and where non-surgical treatment is no longer providing enough relief.

It is generally the right option when:

  • ✓ Hip arthritis limits your walking distance
  • ✓ Avascular necrosis has progressed to collapse of the femoral head
  • ✓ Stiffness makes everyday tasks difficult — socks, shoes, getting into a car
  • ✓ Pain disturbs your sleep
  • ✓ Imaging shows advanced joint damage
  • ✓ Arthritis has developed after an old injury

Where avascular necrosis is caught early , before the femoral head has collapsed, joint-preserving surgery may still be possible — which is why early assessment matters in AVN.

Younger patients are assessed with long-term activity in mind. Where preservation is possible it is considered first, and where replacement is right, components are chosen accordingly.

How robotic assistance changes the procedure

Hip replacement depends heavily on getting three things right.

Cup position

Affects how stable the joint is and how evenly it wears.

Leg length

Affects how you walk and how your back feels afterwards.

Offset

Affects the tension in the surrounding muscles, and therefore both power and stability.

Planning these before surgery and working accurately to that plan is what improves consistency, particularly in complex hips where the anatomy is not standard.

Things to discuss with your surgeon

  • ✓ What results you can realistically expect, given your hip, age and activity
  • ✓ How long the implant is expected to last in your circumstances
  • ✓ Which positions and movements to be mindful of in the early weeks
  • ✓ What the recovery will ask of you, and what support you will need at home
  • ✓ How pain will be managed during your stay and afterwards
  • ✓ How your other medical conditions are managed around surgery
  • ✓ What follow-up looks like, and why long-term review is advised
  • ✓ What the alternatives are, including waiting

Results — what to expect

Hip replacement is among the more reliably successful operations in orthopaedics for pain relief and function.

  • ✓ Walking usually feels natural again once muscle strength returns
  • ✓ Most patients regain the ability to manage stairs, shoes and getting in and out of a car
  • ✓ Sleeping without hip pain is one of the earliest improvements patients notice
  • ✓ Modern implants function well for many years
  • ✓ Low-impact activity is encouraged long term
  • ✓ Long-term follow-up is advised so the joint can be reviewed periodically
