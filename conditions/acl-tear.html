What Is Back Pain?

Back pain is discomfort, aching, stiffness, or pain felt anywhere along the back, most commonly in the lower back. It may occur suddenly after an injury or develop gradually because of posture, physical strain, age-related changes, disc problems, or other spinal conditions.

Back pain may vary

  • ✓ Acute: Develops suddenly and usually lasts for a relatively short period.
  • ✓ Persistent or chronic: Continues or recurs over a longer period and may require medical evaluation.
  • ✓ Radiating: Travels from the back into the buttock, hip, or leg and may be associated with nerve irritation or compression.

The appropriate treatment depends on identifying the underlying cause rather than treating the pain alone.

What Causes Back Pain?

Back pain can have several causes. Some are related to muscles and lifestyle, while others involve the discs, nerves, joints, or spinal canal.

1. Muscle Strain or Ligament Injury

Sudden movements, heavy lifting, overuse, sports injuries, or poor body mechanics can strain the muscles and ligaments supporting the spine. This may cause localised pain, stiffness, and difficulty with movement.

2. Slipped Disc or Herniated Disc

The discs between the vertebrae act as cushions for the spine. When a disc bulges or herniates, it may irritate or compress a nearby nerve. Symptoms may include:

  • ✓ Lower back pain & leg pain
  • ✓ Tingling or pins-and-needles
  • ✓ Numbness & muscle weakness

3. Sciatica

Sciatica occurs when the sciatic nerve or its nerve roots are irritated or compressed. Pain may travel from the lower back or buttocks and travel down the leg. Depending on the cause, sciatica may also cause numbness, tingling, or weakness.

4. Spinal Stenosis

Spinal stenosis refers to narrowing of spaces within the spine that can place pressure on nerves. It is more common with increasing age and may cause back or leg pain, numbness, weakness, or difficulty walking for some patients.

5. Poor Posture and Prolonged Sitting

Long periods of sitting, inadequate workspace ergonomics, reduced physical activity, and poor posture may contribute to back discomfort and place additional stress on the muscles and spinal structures.

6. Age-Related Spine Changes

Disc degeneration, arthritis, and changes in the spinal joints can develop with age. Some people have these changes without symptoms, while others may experience pain, stiffness, or nerve-related symptoms.

7. Spine Injuries

Falls, road traffic accidents, sports injuries, and other trauma can cause injuries to the muscles, discs, vertebrae, or other spinal structures.

When Should You See a Back Pain Doctor?

Not every episode of back pain requires specialist treatment. However, you should consider a medical evaluation if your pain persists, keeps returning, or affects your daily activities.

  • ✓ Persistent or recurrent pain
  • ✓ Interferes with work, sleep, exercise, or daily activities
  • ✓ Is severe or progressively worsening
  • ✓ Travels into the buttock or leg
  • ✓ Is associated with numbness or tingling
  • ✓ Causes weakness in the legs
  • ✓ Makes walking or movement difficult
  • ✓ Develops after a significant injury or accident
  • ✓ Does not improve with appropriate initial care

A spine specialist can determine whether your symptoms are related to muscle, discs, nerves, joints, or other spinal structures.

When Is Back Pain an Emergency?

Certain symptoms accompanying back pain require urgent medical attention. Seek urgent medical evaluation if back pain is associated with:

  • ✓ New or significant weakness in one or both legs
  • ✓ Loss of bladder or bowel control
  • ✓ Numbness around the groin or inner thigh region
  • ✓ Severe symptoms following a major accident or injury
  • ✓ Rapidly worsening neurological symptoms
  • ✓ Difficulty standing or walking because of sudden weakness

These symptoms can indicate significant nerve involvement or another serious condition that requires prompt assessment.

Back Pain Diagnosis in Mumbai

An accurate diagnosis is an important first step in deciding the appropriate treatment for back pain. During a spine consultation, Dr. Vishal Kundnani evaluates your symptoms, medical history, physical findings, and available imaging.

Medical History

The consultation may include questions about when the pain started, its location and severity, previous treatment, associated symptoms, and activities that affect the pain.

Physical Examination

Depending on the symptoms, the examination may assess spinal movement, muscle strength, sensation, reflexes, walking pattern, and signs of nerve irritation or compression.

Imaging and Diagnostic Tests

Investigations are recommended when clinically appropriate. Depending on the suspected cause, these may include X-ray, MRI, CT scan, and nerve-related tests in selected cases.

Not every patient with back pain requires an MRI or other imaging test. The need for investigations depends on the symptoms and clinical examination.

Back Pain Treatment in Mumbai

Treatment for back pain depends on the underlying cause, severity of symptoms, duration of the problem, and presence or absence of neurological symptoms.

Non-Surgical Treatment

Many patients with common, uncomplicated back pain can initially be managed without surgery.

  • ✓ Physiotherapy and exercises prescribed according to symptoms and physical requirements
  • ✓ Medication when appropriate to reduce pain and inflammation
  • ✓ Activity modification and advice on maintaining appropriate movement
  • ✓ Posture and ergonomic guidance
  • ✓ Weight and lifestyle management where appropriate

The goal is to reduce symptoms, improve movement, and support a gradual return to normal activities.

Minimally Invasive Spine Treatment

When symptoms do not improve with appropriate non-surgical treatment, or when a specific spinal condition requires a procedure, minimally invasive techniques may be considered where appropriate.

Depending on the diagnosis, treatment may include:

  • ✓ Endoscopic spine procedures
  • ✓ Minimally invasive discectomy
  • ✓ Nerve decompression procedures
  • ✓ Other minimally invasive spinal procedures
  • ✓ Spine stabilisation procedures when clinically indicated

Minimally invasive techniques use smaller approaches in selected procedures and may reduce tissue disruption. The suitability of a particular technique depends on the patient's diagnosis, anatomy, symptoms, and overall health.

Back Pain Conditions Treated by a Spine Specialist

A spine specialist may evaluate and treat a range of conditions associated with back pain, including:

  • ✓ Slipped or herniated disc
  • ✓ Sciatica
  • ✓ Spinal stenosis
  • ✓ Degenerative disc disease
  • ✓ Spinal arthritis
  • ✓ Nerve compression
  • ✓ Certain spinal injuries
  • ✓ Other conditions affecting the lumbar and spinal structures

The treatment approach is based on the specific diagnosis rather than the presence of back pain alone.

Back Pain Doctor vs Orthopaedic Doctor: Whom Should You Consult?

Back pain can have many causes, and the appropriate specialist depends on the symptoms and suspected condition.

For persistent, radiating, severe, or nerve-related back pain, a spine specialist or spine surgeon can provide a focused evaluation of the spinal structures, nerves, discs, and related conditions.

If your symptoms are mild and recent, your primary doctor or an appropriate musculoskeletal healthcare professional may also be able to provide an initial assessment.

If symptoms persist, recur, worsen, or are associated with neurological symptoms, specialist evaluation may be appropriate.
