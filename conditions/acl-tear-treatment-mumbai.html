ACL Tear
Treatment in Mumbai

Introduction

The anterior cruciate ligament, or ACL, is one of the main stabilising ligaments of the knee. It is most commonly torn during a sudden twist, pivot or landing in cricket, football, badminton, kabaddi and gym training, and also in ordinary slips and road accidents.

Dr. Vaibhav Bagaria has treated ACL injuries in professional athletes and recreational players for over two decades at Sir H. N. Reliance Foundation Hospital, Mumbai. Not every ACL tear needs surgery — the decision depends on your age, your activity, whether the knee is giving way, and what else was injured at the same time.

What is an ACL tear?

The ACL runs diagonally through the centre of the knee, connecting the thigh bone to the shin bone. Its job is to stop the shin sliding forward and to control rotation. When it tears, the knee loses that rotational control.

Tears are graded by severity. A partial tear leaves some fibres intact. A complete tear separates the ligament entirely — this is the most common presentation and the ACL does not heal back together on its own. ACL injuries frequently occur alongside meniscus tears, cartilage damage or injury to other ligaments.

Symptoms of an ACL tear

  • ✓ A popping sound or sensation at the moment of injury
  • ✓ Immediate swelling, usually within a few hours
  • ✓ Severe pain at the time, which may settle over days
  • ✓ Inability to continue playing
  • ✓ A feeling that the knee is unstable or "giving way"
  • ✓ Difficulty pivoting, turning or changing direction
  • ✓ Reduced ability to fully straighten or bend the knee
  • ✓ Recurrent giving way months later, even when pain has settled

Swelling within a few hours of a twisting injury is significant — it suggests bleeding inside the joint and warrants assessment.

What causes an ACL tear?

1. Pivoting and twisting

Changing direction with the foot planted, the most common mechanism.

2. Awkward landing

Landing from a jump with the knee straight or turning inward.

3. Sudden deceleration

Stopping abruptly at speed.

4. Direct impact

A blow to the outside of the knee, common in contact sport.

5. Road traffic accidents

Particularly two-wheeler injuries.

6. Everyday slips

Missing a step or slipping on a wet surface.

When should you see a doctor?

  • ✓ Your knee swelled within hours of a twisting injury
  • ✓ You heard or felt a pop at the time
  • ✓ The knee gives way during turning or on uneven ground
  • ✓ You cannot fully straighten the knee
  • ✓ You cannot return to sport with confidence
  • ✓ Symptoms have not settled after two weeks

When is a knee injury an emergency?

Seek urgent care for obvious deformity, inability to bear any weight, numbness or coldness below the knee, or a knee that appears dislocated. Multi-ligament injuries can affect blood vessels and nerves and need immediate assessment.

How an ACL tear is diagnosed

Medical history

How the injury happened, whether there was a pop, how quickly swelling appeared, whether you could continue, and whether the knee has given way since.

Physical examination

Specific stability tests including the Lachman and pivot-shift tests, alongside assessment of swelling, range of movement, the other ligaments, and signs of meniscus injury.

Imaging

X-rays exclude fracture. MRI confirms the ACL tear and, importantly, identifies associated meniscus and cartilage injuries that affect the treatment plan.

ACL tear treatment in Mumbai

Non-surgical treatment

Suitable for selected patients — typically those with partial tears, lower activity demands, or no instability in daily life:

  • ✓ Structured physiotherapy focused on quadriceps and hamstring strength
  • ✓ Neuromuscular and balance training
  • ✓ Activity modification, avoiding pivoting sports
  • ✓ Bracing in selected cases

When surgery is considered

ACL reconstruction is usually recommended for patients who want to return to pivoting sports, who experience recurrent giving way, who have associated meniscus or ligament injuries requiring repair, or who have physically demanding occupations.

Repeated episodes of giving way damage the meniscus and cartilage over time, which is a significant reason to treat instability rather than live with it.

ACL reconstruction

The torn ligament is replaced with a graft, positioned arthroscopically through small incisions. Arthroscopic ACL reconstruction allows any meniscus or cartilage injury to be addressed at the same time.

Recovery is staged , not fixed to a date. Early rehabilitation restores movement and quadriceps control; strength work follows; running is introduced when specific strength and control targets are met; and return to pivoting sport comes last, typically after several months, once objective criteria are satisfied. Returning before the knee is ready is a leading cause of re-injury.

Frequently Asked Questions

A completely torn ACL does not heal back together. Some patients manage well without reconstruction through rehabilitation, particularly if they avoid pivoting activity and the knee does not give way. Surgery is recommended where instability affects daily life or sport.
Surgery is usually performed once swelling has settled and movement has been restored, which typically takes a few weeks. Operating on a stiff, swollen knee increases the risk of stiffness afterwards. Associated injuries may change this timing.
Return to pivoting sport typically takes several months and depends on meeting strength and control targets rather than reaching a date. Your surgeon and physiotherapist will assess readiness objectively.
Most patients return to their previous activities. Outcomes depend on the quality of rehabilitation, associated injuries, and following the graduated return-to-sport plan.
Repeated giving way can damage the meniscus and cartilage, which increases the risk of arthritis later. Patients who avoid pivoting activity and have a stable knee may do well, but persistent instability should be assessed.
An experienced examination can strongly suggest an ACL tear, but MRI confirms it and identifies associated meniscus and cartilage injuries which materially affect the treatment plan.
