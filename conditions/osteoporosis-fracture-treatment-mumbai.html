Osteoporosis & Fragility Fracture
Treatment in Mumbai

Introduction

Osteoporosis weakens bone to the point where a fall from standing height or sometimes no fall at all can cause a fracture. Hip, wrist and spinal fractures are the most common results, and a hip fracture in an older adult is a serious event that affects independence.

Dr. Vaibhav Bagaria treats fragility fractures at Sir H. N. Reliance Foundation Hospital, Mumbai. Fixing the fracture is only half the task; the other half is reducing the chance of the next one.

What is osteoporosis?

Bone is living tissue, continually being broken down and rebuilt. In osteoporosis, breakdown outpaces rebuilding, so bone becomes less dense and its internal structure thins. The bone looks normal from outside but fractures under loads it should tolerate.

A fragility fracture is one occurring from a fall at standing height or less — a level of force that would not normally break bone. The most common sites are the hip, the wrist and the spine.

Osteoporosis itself causes no symptoms until a fracture occurs, which is why it is often described as silent. A first fragility fracture significantly increases the risk of a second, so it should be treated as a warning rather than an isolated accident.

Symptoms

  • ✓ A fracture from a minor fall or low-impact injury
  • ✓ Loss of height over time
  • ✓ A stooped or rounded upper back
  • ✓ Sudden back pain, which may indicate a spinal compression fracture
  • ✓ Back pain after lifting or a minor jolt
  • ✓ Fractures occurring more easily than expected
  • ✓ Wrist fracture after a simple fall onto an outstretched hand

Who is at risk?

1. Age

bone density declines with age in everyone.

2. Post-menopausal women

bone loss accelerates after menopause, though osteoporosis also affects men.

3. Long-term steroid use

a significant and well-recognised risk factor.

4. Low body weight and poor nutrition

including inadequate calcium and vitamin D.

5. Vitamin D deficiency

widespread in India despite abundant sunlight, and an important contributor.

6. Family history

particularly of hip fracture in a parent.

7. Smoking and alcohol

8. Inactivity

weight-bearing activity helps maintain bone density.

9. Medical conditions

including thyroid disorders, rheumatoid arthritis and conditions affecting nutrient absorption.

When should you see a doctor?

  • ✓ After any fracture from a minor fall
  • ✓ Sudden back pain in an older adult
  • ✓ Noticeable height loss or increasing stoop
  • ✓ Long-term steroid treatment
  • ✓ Post-menopausal with risk factors
  • ✓ A parent who had a hip fracture

When is a fracture an emergency?

Seek urgent care for inability to bear weight after a fall, a leg that appears shortened or rotated, severe pain after a fall in an older adult, sudden severe back pain, or any back pain accompanied by leg weakness, numbness, or loss of bladder or bowel control.

How osteoporosis and fragility fractures are assessed

Fracture assessment

X-rays of the affected site. CT or MRI where the fracture is not clearly visible, which is not uncommon in hip and spinal fractures in fragile bone.

Bone health assessment

DEXA scan — measures bone density and is the standard test

Blood tests — including vitamin D, calcium and thyroid function

Fracture risk assessment — combining density and clinical risk factors

Spinal imaging — where compression fractures are suspected

Falls assessment

Reviewing balance, vision, medication, footwear and home hazards, since preventing the fall prevents the fracture.

Osteoporosis and fragility fracture treatment in Mumbai

Fracture treatment

Most patients are managed without surgery. Treatment is adjusted to the stage of the condition and the level of pain and stiffness:

  • ✓ Hip fracture surgery — fixation or replacement depending on the fracture pattern and the patient, with the aim of getting the patient standing and moving as soon as it is safe
  • ✓ Wrist fracture — plaster or surgical fixation depending on displacement
  • ✓ Spinal compression fracture — usually managed non-surgically with pain relief, bracing and mobilisation; procedures are considered in selected cases
  • ✓ Surgery in fragile bone — requires implants and techniques suited to poor bone quality

Bone health treatment

Surgery may be considered when significant stiffness persists despite appropriate non-surgical treatment for several months and continues to interfere with work, sleep or everyday activities.

Medical management of osteoporosis is often shared with a physician or endocrinologist.
